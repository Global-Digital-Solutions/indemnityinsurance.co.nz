## A. WHAT IS *NOT* REQUIRED (verified negatives — high value, nobody else publishes these)
- **Financial advice providers (FAPs):** PI is NOT an FMA licence condition. The seven standard conditions are record keeping, internal complaints process, regulatory returns, outsourcing, business continuity and technology systems, ongoing requirements, notification of material changes. The FMA *consulted* on adding PI (17 June 2020) and **decided against it** (6 November 2020). Not in the Code of Professional Conduct (v2, in force 1 Nov 2025). No PI disclosure obligation.
- **Real estate agents:** REA does NOT require PI. The Professional Conduct and Client Care Rules 2012 contain zero occurrences of "insurance" or "indemnity". REA licensing criteria are age, fit and proper, NZ Certificate in Real Estate, criminal history, referees. Trust account obligations are AUDIT only (Real Estate Agents (Audit) Regulations 2009) — no PI, no fidelity insurance.
- **Architects:** the Registered Architects Act 2005 contains no insurance provisions. Registered Architects Rules 2006 mention PI only at rule 50, as a permissive item in agreed client terms of appointment. Continuing registration = 5-yearly competence review + CPD. The statutory instrument is a "certificate of registration", NOT an "annual practising certificate". **There is no "Architects Act 2022".**
- **Engineers:** Engineering NZ does NOT require PI for membership or for CPEng. CPEng = Washington Accord-accredited qualification or equivalent, competence assessment, Code of Ethical Conduct, reassessment at least every 6 years. Chartered Professional Engineers of NZ Rules (No 2) 2002 contain no occurrence of "indemnity" or "insurance".
- **Nurses:** Nursing Council does NOT require PI for an APC (continuing competence + annual renewal + fee NZ$32.50–$130.00). College of Nurses Aotearoa PI is a member BENEFIT, not a requirement.
- **Social workers:** SWRB practising certificate does NOT require PI (annual renewal, Code of Conduct, CPD, online competence/fitness declaration, expires 30 June).
- **Midwives:** NZ College of Midwives PI is an automatic BENEFIT of membership, not a requirement. (DHBs require midwives holding an Access Agreement to maintain it — that is a third-party contractual requirement.)

## C. THE ONLY CITABLE PREMIUM FIGURES IN NEW ZEALAND
Attribute every one of these inline. Do not restate them as "typical NZ premiums" generally.

1. **$1,500–$3,000 per person per year** — PI for **architects** (and design professionals). NZ Government.
   - Beehive factsheet, 24 Nov 2025: "Professional indemnity insurance for architects typically costs between $1,500 and $3,000 per person each year, often covered by the firm."
   - Cabinet paper, 18 Nov 2025, para 54: "The cost of PI cover varies depending on work undertaken but typically ranges from $1,500–$3,000 per year."
   - Caveat to state: a government estimate published to support a policy decision, with no methodology attached.
2. **NZ Law Society / KPMG, *Benchmarking costs of law practice in New Zealand*** (n=124 firms, surveyed Oct–Nov 2023, published March 2024). Average PI cost **per firm**, 2023: barrister **$2,850**; sole practitioner **$7,227**; small firm 2–19 lawyers **$66,044**; medium 20–49 **$100,996**; large 50+ **$306,458**. PI grew **+23.8% CAGR 2021–2023**, the highest of any overhead category; small firms **+33.1%**. Caveats: law only, per firm not per person, 2023 data.
3. **NZMII published medical indemnity scheme prices** (undated fact sheet, references the 2025 HDC report): private sector / GPEP RMOs **$565–$600/year**; Senior Doctors / SMOs **$1,621.50/year**; Health NZ hospital RMOs at no personal cost. Group scheme medical indemnity, not general PI.
4. Context, official: around **90%** of designers and engineers already hold PI voluntarily. Home warranties estimated at around **0.5% of total build cost**.

**Everything else must be deleted.** There is no public NZ PI premium dataset. Delta Insurance and Vero Liability (the two main NZ PI underwriters) publish no pricing — cover is broker-distributed and individually underwritten. Engineering NZ, NZIA, ADNZ, CA ANZ publish no premiums. The NZ Law Society's own March 2026 consultation on compulsory PI discusses cost increases and gives **zero** dollar premium figures. Marsh's Q1 2026 index has no NZ PI breakout (Pacific bundles Australia with NZ).

## D. HOW TO WRITE ABOUT COST INSTEAD
Explain the rating factors an underwriter actually uses, then route to a quote:
- **Limit of indemnity** — anchor to the real published minimums in section B ($200k producer statements, $250k ADNZ, $1M REINZ, $1.2m Law Society).
- **Fee income / revenue** — the primary rating base for most PI.
- **Work type and discipline** — residential construction, structural and geotechnical work rate materially harder. Describe qualitatively; do not attach a number.
- **Claims and notification history.**
- **Excess / deductible** — Law Society standard: not more than the greater of 1% of limit or $20,000.
- **Retroactive date and run-off** — PI is claims-made, so prior-acts cover is a genuine price lever.
- **Defence costs inside or outside the limit.**
- **Contractual requirements** — clients and councils frequently set the limit, which sets the price.
Say plainly: NZ PI is broker-distributed and individually underwritten, no insurer publishes rate tables, and the only official cost guidance is the Government's $1,500–$3,000 per person per year for design professionals.

## E. THE BUILDING REFORM — accurate position as at 7 September 2026
- Announced **24 November 2025** by Hon Chris Penk, Minister for Building and Construction.
- PI limb: professionals contributing to building design — architects and engineers — to hold PI. Factsheet scope: "architecture, design, engineering and certain building surveying services". Expressly **"It will not apply to builders."** Not BCAs. Not developers. LBPs are not required to carry insurance under the proposed amendments (Simpson Grierson).
- Warranty limb: new residential up to 10 metres (announcement said "three storeys and under"; Bill as introduced says 10m — use the Bill wording), renovations **$100,000+**, 1-year defect period, 10-year structural warranty.
- **NOT LAW.** Building Amendment Bill introduced **2 July 2026**, before select committee, submissions close **11.59pm Sunday 15 November 2026**.
- **No minimum limit proposed.** MBIE RIS 18 Nov 2025: "The details of this scheme are yet to be developed and require further consultation." Simpson Grierson: absent a legislative minimum, "it is likely the insurers will have the final say."
- **Expected to take effect 2028**, after a one-year lead-in once the Bill passes. NOT 2026.
- No official document states any required duration of cover or run-off obligation.
- Unverified lead: reports that the Bill contains a power to temporarily suspend the requirement. Do not assert.

## F. LIMITATION PERIODS — state these correctly, they are routinely wrong
- Primary period: **6 years, section 11 Limitation Act 2010** — NOT the Building Act — running from the **act or omission**, not from practical or substantial completion.
- **Late knowledge extension: 3 years, section 14 Limitation Act 2010**, from when the claimant knew or ought reasonably to have known.
- **Section 393 Building Act 2004: absolute 10-year longstop** on civil proceedings relating to building work, also running from the **act or omission**. It is a hard stop, not a "10-year latent defects period".
